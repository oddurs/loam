## Context

With two programs holding written context in one repository, something has to
say which one a given piece of writing belongs in. If nothing does, both fill
with the same things and neither can be trusted to be complete.

## Options

- By author: people write docs, agents write items. (Arbitrary, and false today.)
- By length: short in cairn, long in docs. (cairn's items already run ninety lines.)
- **By tense.**

## Recommendation

**A cairn item records why something changed. A page says how it is now.**

cairn's items are worth more after they close because they answer *why is it
like this* (cairn `0071`). A page is worth something only while it is current.
One is history written at the moment of deciding; the other is a description
kept true.

Three seam rules follow:

1. **A question is an item; its answer, if it outlives the question, is a page.**
   harrow's `0022` is the pattern: the spike is opened, worked and closed in
   cairn. A finding longer than a paragraph goes to `docs/research/`, and the
   item links to it.
2. **A plan with steps that will be finished is backlog.** A milestone's body
   plus its items *is* the plan. A "plan" that describes the shape of the thing —
   measure-of-the-world's chapter outlines — is design, and is a page.
3. **A page cites the items that made it how it is.** The design page says what
   the design is; the decisions behind it are linked, not restated.

## Consequences

- `docs/plan/PLAN.md`-style files mostly stop existing; their content is either
  a milestone or a design page.
- Where a decision record lives is the hard case, and is its own decision.

## Applied to the three real folders

Every file in the three docs folders (69, as of the commits in
`spec/corpus/NOTICE.md`), sorted by the three rules.

| Folder | Page: how it is now | Backlog: why it changed, or steps to finish | Neither |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| code-as-color (17) | 10: README, architecture, authoring, concept, design, outline, releases, research, voice, writing-process | 6: the five ADRs in `decisions/` and their README (see 0015) | 1: `roadmap-intro.md`, the header `cairn render` puts on ROADMAP.md |
| measure-of-the-world (15) | 12: build, contributing, releases, quality, styleguide, and in `plan/` the outlines, prompts, foreword, acknowledgements and global guide — rule 2: the shape of the book, not steps | 2: `publication.md`, a checklist to v2.0.0, is a milestone; `plan/production-engine.md` splits — its passes are milestones, its engine layout and principles are a design page | 1: `status.md`, generated by `make status` |
| poptop (37) | 29: README, and every page in guide/, reference/ and design/ | 8: `roadmaps/`, numbered steps marked DONE. poptop already moved their tracking to cairn and kept the files as reasoning, which is the rule arrived at independently | 0 |

The rules held: no file needed a fourth rule to say which side it belonged on,
and the one that splits, `production-engine.md`, splits along the line rule 2
draws. What they do not cover is files that are not written context at all —
an input to another tool, and a generated report. Those are not a failure of
the tense rule but a thing loam's index and staleness check will meet, filed
as their own question.

## What it rules out

- Dividing by author, by length, or by where a file happens to be today.
- Plans in the docs folder. A plan is a milestone and its items.
- Decision records in the docs folder as the record; see 0015.
