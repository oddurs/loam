# Concept

## The thesis

Light arriving at the eye is a function of wavelength — a point in an
infinite-dimensional space. The eye reports three numbers. Any such map has an
enormous null space, and the practical consequences of that null space are what
this subject is.

That sentence is discharged by Chapter 2. Everything after it is consequence,
and by Part IV the spine has stopped carrying load and the book becomes a
reference work with a point of view. **That is a deliberate acceptance, not an
oversight.** The spine's job is to order the material, not to sustain suspense.

## Who it is for

Readers comfortable with matrices, change of basis, and integrals as inner
products, who can read code, and who have no prior colorimetry. Graphics
programmers, data-visualisation people, shader authors, and front-end developers
who have just met `oklch()` in CSS and want to know what it is.

## The claim we are allowed to make

Not "nothing exists for this reader" — Poynton, Fairchild, Reinhard and
Ottosson's free posts all exist, and are good. The honest claim is narrower:

> Nothing exists at this altitude, in one place, with the derivations shown.

## What the book is not

- **Not a colour-design book.** It will not tell you which blue to use.
- **Not an appearance-modelling monograph.** CAM16 gets a section, not a part.
- **Not interactive.** That tension is real: the deepest version of this book
  would let the reader drag a slider and watch the gamut boundary move. We chose
  print. The repository is the compromise — every figure is executable, and
  disagreeing with one means editing it and rebuilding.

## The commitment that is infrastructure, not marketing

"Nothing is transcribed" is an author's virtue. It buys *trust*, and it should
be invisible: readers care about clarity, not provenance. Do not lead with it.
